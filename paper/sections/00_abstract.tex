\begin{abstract}
Learning cooperation in tightly coupled multiagent tasks is difficult because
useful individual behavior may yield little reward until teammates discover
complementary behavior. When success requires coordinated physical interaction
over multiple timesteps, agents may rarely encounter examples of cooperation
early in training. Feudal hierarchies structure exploration through
manager-assigned goals, but discovering the joint behavior needed to achieve
those goals remains a challenge. We introduce a heuristic for training interventions that creates opportunities for cooperation by placing existing teammates next to a focal agent and aligning their goals. These counterfactual configurations are instantiated in simulator
rollouts, and the resulting trajectories provide additional experience for
training both manager and worker policies using the existing reward signals. Our key insight is that the teammate-placement heuristic accelerates learning by generating cooperative experiences that would
otherwise depend on agents independently discovering compatible positions and goals. We evaluate our interventions on a continuous-control cooperative object pushing task. Where we show that interventions lead to faster convergence under the same training budget when compared to state of the art baselines.
\end{abstract}
